# Ruiz Legal — Full Marketing & Ad Campaign Strategy

**Client:** Santino Ruiz, Esq. — Ruiz Legal (ruizlegal.law)
**Market:** South Florida (Miami / Fort Lauderdale / West Palm Beach), statewide FL
**Practice:** Personal injury, vehicle accidents, first-party insurance claims / bad faith
**Brand assets:** Gold "RL" monogram, black/white/gold palette, B&W editorial portrait of Santino
**Languages:** English + Spanish (every concept below should ship in both)
**Prepared:** August 2026

---

## 1. Brand Positioning

**Core message (the spine of the whole campaign):**

> **"The first offer is not the best offer."**
> Insurance companies make fast, low offers because unrepresented people accept them.
> Ruiz Legal exists to make sure you never negotiate alone.

**Positioning statement:** Ruiz Legal is the South Florida firm that knows the insurance playbook from the inside — and turns it against the carriers. Not a billboard mill, not a settlement factory: one relentless, bilingual attorney who picks up the phone himself.

**Tone:** Confident, direct, protective. Editorial black & white + gold accent. Never ambulance-chaser loud; always "the adult in the room who fights."

**Differentiators to hammer in every asset:**
1. First-offer education angle (unique — most PI firms shout settlement numbers instead)
2. Insurance-law depth (first-party/bad-faith — he knows how carriers think)
3. Direct attorney access (you talk to Ruiz, not a case manager)
4. Bilingual, 24/7
5. Contingency fee ("no fees unless we win" — always with the required costs disclaimer, see §4)

**Audience segments:**
- A. Recent auto/rideshare accident victims (0–14 days post-crash, highest intent)
- B. People already holding a lowball offer or denied claim (the "second opinion" market)
- C. Spanish-first households in Miami-Dade/Broward (underserved by English-only firms)
- D. Homeowners/policyholders fighting their own carrier (hurricane season spikes)

## 2. The 10 Ad Concepts

### Concept 1 — "The First Offer Is Not The Best Offer" *(flagship)*
**Strategy:** Education-as-ambush. Instead of "were you injured?", attack the exact moment of maximum leverage: the victim staring at the insurer's first check. Positions signing fast as the costly mistake.

- **Hook:** "That first check? It's not a gift. It's a strategy."
- **Headlines:**
  - EN: **"The first offer is never their best offer."**
  - ES: **"La primera oferta nunca es su mejor oferta."**
- **Primary copy:** Insurance companies resolve claims quickly for one reason: it saves *them* money. Before you sign anything, know what your case may actually be worth. Free consultation. No fees or costs unless we win — you remain responsible for costs and expenses only if we recover for you.
- **CTA:** "Get a free case review before you sign." / 305-771-6801
- **Placements:** Meta feed + IG Reels cover, Google Search ("should I accept insurance settlement offer"), YouTube pre-roll, billboard on I-95/826.
- **Visual:** Santino in black suit, arms crossed (real photo), beside an oversized settlement check stamped **"THINK TWICE"** or being slid back across a table. Black background, gold accents.
- **Compliance:** No dollar amounts, no outcome promises. "May actually be worth" not "will get you more." Contingency disclaimer required (§4.3).

### Concept 2 — "Know What It's Really Worth"
**Strategy:** The iceberg visual — the visible check vs. the submerged real damages (future medical, lost wages, pain and suffering). Converts segment B (already holding an offer).

- **Hook:** "Their offer covers the ER visit. What about the next five years?"
- **Headlines:** EN: **"An injury claim is more than one hospital bill."** / ES: **"Su reclamo vale más que una factura de hospital."**
- **Primary copy:** Future treatment. Lost income. The life you had before. A first offer rarely accounts for all of it. Before you settle, let us evaluate everything your claim may include — free.
- **CTA:** "Free claim evaluation → ruizlegal.law"
- **Placements:** Meta carousel (each card = one hidden damage category), LinkedIn, Google Display retargeting.
- **Visual:** Iceberg metaphor — small check above the waterline, the full claim below; black water, gold linework.
- **Compliance:** "May include" language; no valuation promises.

### Concept 3 — "We Speak Insurance" *(bilingual power play)*
**Strategy:** Own the language angle twice: Spanish for the community, "insurance-ese" as the third language nobody else speaks.

- **Hook:** "Hablamos español. Hablamos inglés. Y hablamos el idioma de las aseguradoras."
- **Headlines:** EN: **"We speak fluent insurance."** / ES: **"Hablamos el idioma de su aseguradora."**
- **Primary copy:** Exclusions, riders, PIP, UM/UIM — the policy is written so you won't read it. Santino Ruiz built his practice on first-party insurance law. Bring us the fine print. Consultas gratis, en español, 24/7.
- **CTA:** "Llámenos hoy / Call today — 305-771-6801"
- **Placements:** Spanish-language radio (La Poderosa, El Zol), Univision/Telemundo local pre-roll, Meta ES-language targeting Miami-Dade, WhatsApp click-to-chat ads.
- **Visual:** Magnifying glass held over dense policy fine print, gold light revealing the word "EXCLUSIÓN"; alternate: split-type headline EN/ES.
- **Compliance:** Spanish ads must carry Spanish disclaimers (§4.7). "Built his practice on" is fine; do NOT say "specialist/expert in insurance law" unless board-certified (§4.5).

### Concept 4 — "They Have Adjusters. You Have Ruiz."
**Strategy:** David-vs-Goliath asymmetry. Name the machine (adjusters, defense counsel, algorithms) and sell the equalizer.

- **Hook:** "The insurance company assigned five people to your claim. How many do you have?"
- **Headlines:** EN: **"They have a team. Now you do too."** / ES: **"Ellos tienen un equipo. Ahora usted también."**
- **Primary copy:** Adjusters, defense lawyers, claim software — all working to close your file cheap. You're allowed to bring your own fighter. Direct access to attorney Santino Ruiz. Not a call center.
- **CTA:** "Talk to the attorney — today."
- **Placements:** YouTube 15s pre-roll, OTT/streaming (Hulu/Roku Miami DMA), Meta video.
- **Visual:** Santino standing alone, lit in gold, facing a wall of grey corporate silhouettes/towers. Cinematic, low-key lighting.
- **Compliance:** Fine as comparison of resources, not of results. Avoid "we always win" implications.

### Concept 5 — "After the Storm" *(seasonal — hurricane/property + injury)*
**Strategy:** June–November surge campaign for South Florida. First-party claim denials spike after storms; Ruiz's bad-faith practice is the perfect fit. Also catches injury claims from storm cleanup.

- **Hook:** "The storm lasted six hours. The claim fight lasts six months — unless."
- **Headlines:** EN: **"When the storm passes, the real fight begins."** / ES: **"Después de la tormenta, empieza la verdadera lucha."**
- **Primary copy:** Underpaid. Delayed. Denied. Florida insurers have a playbook for storm season — so do we. If your claim was lowballed or denied, you may have more options than they told you.
- **CTA:** "Denied or underpaid? Free policy review."
- **Placements:** Geo-triggered Meta/Google within 72h of named-storm landfall zones, local radio, direct response landing page.
- **Visual:** Dramatic Florida storm sky clearing over a damaged roofline, gold sunrise breaking through; RL logo as the constant.
- **Compliance:** Truthful "may have more options" framing. No solicitation via direct in-person/phone contact of storm victims; paid ads are fine, targeted direct mail must respect 4-7.18 (30-day rule doesn't apply to first-party property claims against victims' own insurer the way it does to PI defendants, but keep mail compliant and filed).

### Concept 6 — "Hit in a Rideshare?" *(niche domination)*
**Strategy:** Own the confusing-liability niches (Uber/Lyft, hit-and-run, uninsured drivers) where DIY claimants are most lost. High-intent search terms, low competition vs. generic "car accident lawyer."

- **Placements:** Google Search exact-match rideshare terms, IG Reels/TikTok (skit format: three adjusters pointing at each other), waiting-room screens at urgent cares.
- **Visual:** Night-time Miami street, rain-slicked neon, rideshare car with hazards on; three arrows pointing away from it. Cinematic.
- **Compliance:** If any actor portrays a client/driver in video, add "DRAMATIZATION" super (§4.6).

### Concept 7 — "No Fees Unless We Win" *(risk-reversal, done legally)*
**Strategy:** Everyone says it; almost nobody explains it. Convert by *demystifying* the contingency fee — transparency as the differentiator.

- **Hook:** "What does 'no fees unless we win' actually mean? Let's do the math in 30 seconds."
- **Headlines:** EN: **"You don't pay us. The outcome does."** / ES: **"Usted no nos paga. Paga el resultado."**
- **Primary copy:** No upfront costs. No hourly bills. Our fee is a percentage only if we recover for you. Clients remain responsible for costs and expenses. We'll explain every line before you sign — that's the point of us.
- **CTA:** "Ask us anything — free consult."
- **Placements:** IG/TikTok explainer video (Santino to camera), Google Search "how much does a personal injury lawyer cost," FAQ-schema landing page.
- **Visual:** Santino at a desk, whiteboard-style gold typography explaining the fee simply; honest, no props.
- **Compliance:** MUST carry the costs/expenses disclaimer in the same breath (§4.3). This concept is literally built around the disclaimer — it becomes a trust asset.

## 4. Florida Bar Advertising Compliance (Rules 4-7.11 – 4-7.23)

Everything above was written to be compliant, but these are the hard rules for production and trafficking. Source: Florida Bar Handbook on Lawyer Advertising and Solicitation (rev. Dec 2025) and Rule 4-7 series.

1. **Name + geography on every ad (4-7.12):** Every advertisement must include the firm name ("Ruiz Legal") and the city of a bona fide office ("Miami, FL"). Put it on end cards, footers, and radio tags.
2. **No false, misleading, or unverifiable claims (4-7.13):** No promised outcomes, no "we win or it's free" implications about results, no unverifiable comparisons ("best," "most aggressive in Florida"). Objective, verifiable statements only.
3. **Contingency fee disclaimer (4-7.14):** Any "no fees unless we win" statement must disclose that the client is responsible for **costs and expenses** (and whether that applies regardless of outcome, per the firm's actual fee contract). Same size/prominence rules apply — "clear and conspicuous."
4. **Past results (4-7.13/4-7.14):** If any ad ever cites recoveries ("Millions recovered"), it must be objectively verifiable and carry **"Prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome."** None of the 10 concepts rely on results figures — deliberately.
5. **"Specialist/Expert" (4-7.14(a)(4)):** Only if board certified by The Florida Bar in that area. Until then: "focuses his practice on," "built his practice on."
6. **Dramatizations & actors (4-7.15/4-7.13):** Any reenactment or actor portraying clients/events needs a clear **"DRAMATIZATION — NOT AN ACTUAL EVENT"** disclosure; an actor portraying a lawyer requires disclosure too. **This applies to AI-generated imagery:** any AI image/video that could be read as a real client, real accident, or real event must be treated as a dramatization and labeled. AI-generated depictions of Santino himself should not show him doing things he didn't do; composites from his real photo for stylized portraits are fine.
7. **Language parity:** Disclaimers must appear **in the language of the ad** — Spanish ads carry Spanish disclaimers, equally prominent.
8. **Testimonials (4-7.13(b)(8)):** Must be actual clients (or disclose otherwise), can't be paid actors presented as clients, can't convey expected results, and need "This testimonial may not be representative of other clients' experience" wording where required.
9. **Filing requirement (4-7.19/4-7.20):** TV, radio, print, OOH, and most digital display ads must be **filed with The Florida Bar 20 days before first use ($150/ad)** unless they qualify for the safe-harbor exemption (name/contact/practice-area-only ads, and the firm's own website is exempt). Concepts 1–8 and 10 as designed **will require filing** once produced. Budget filing fees and lead time into the production calendar.
10. **Solicitation limits (4-7.18):** No direct in-person, live phone, or real-time electronic solicitation of accident victims. Paid ads and organic content are fine; targeted direct mail to accident victims must wait where the 30-day rule applies (auto accidents/wrongful death) and carry "ADVERTISEMENT" markings.
11. **Recommended micro-footer for all static ads:**
    > Ruiz Legal · Miami, FL · Attorney Santino Ruiz. Free consultation. No fees unless we win; clients are responsible for costs and expenses. The hiring of a lawyer is an important decision that should not be based solely upon advertisements.

*(The "important decision" sentence is no longer strictly mandated in most formats but remains best practice and reads as credibility.)*

## 5. Production Notes for Image/Video Generation

- **Palette:** #000000 / #FFFFFF / gold #C9A227 (sampled from logo). B&W photography + single gold accent is the brand look.
- **Face rule:** Never let a model synthesize Santino's face from a text description — always composite/edit from the real photo (`generations/refs/santino_ruiz_hero.png`, transparent background).
- **Logo rule:** Same — place the real file (`generations/refs/ruiz_legal_logo_dark.png` for light backgrounds, `_light` for dark), never let a model redraw it. Models will mangle the RL monogram.
- **Text in images:** Keep generated-in text to ≤6 words; final type should be set in post (Figma/Photoshop) for legal-disclaimer legibility anyway — disclaimers must be "clear and conspicuous," which generated type can't guarantee.
- **Model notes from the bake-off:** see `generations/ruiz-legal/` sidecar JSONs — every image logs its model, provider, prompt, and refs.
